The Lost Sheep

Luke 15:1–7 · Pastor Naomi Carter · November 8, 2026

Watch the sermon Listen (MP3)

The passage

Suppose one of you has a hundred sheep and loses one of them. Doesn't he leave the ninety-nine in the open country and go after the lost sheep until he finds it? And when he finds it, he joyfully puts it on his shoulders and goes home.

Luke 15:4–6

Summary

Jesus tells this story to people who were annoyed at the company he kept. Tax collectors and other outcasts were crowding in to listen, and the religious leaders muttered that he welcomed them and even ate with them. Jesus answers with a shepherd who does something that makes no business sense.

  1. The shepherd counts. One in a hundred is a rounding error, unless every one has a name. God notices who is missing.
  2. The shepherd goes. He doesn't wait for the sheep to find its way back. The searching is all his.
  3. The shepherd carries. The sheep comes home on his shoulders, not on its own legs, and the story ends with a party.

Questions for small groups

  1. Who would the "tax collectors and sinners" be in our town today? Who would be muttering?
  2. When have you felt like the one, and when like one of the ninety-nine?
  3. The shepherd calls his friends to celebrate. Who could you celebrate with this week?
